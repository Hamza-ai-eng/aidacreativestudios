UNRWA gives you a registration number. The number helps you access services — schools, hospitals, food assistance. Your number is one among roughly one million Palestinians.

But one number is not enough. In 2020, UNRWA decided to add a biometric recognition system — a technology called IrisGuard. The technology photographs your iris — the colored part of your eye. Your iris is unique — like a fingerprint, it does not repeat.

The stated goal is reasonable: verify that the person withdrawing assistance is the same person registered. A secondary goal: if one person is collecting aid under multiple names, detect it.

The method: you go to a UNRWA office. You stand in front of a machine. You let it photograph your iris. The system compares the image to photographs in your file. If it matches — 99 percent accuracy — you receive your assistance.

What is new here: biometric data differs from a signature. A signature — if someone taught you to write it wrong, you can change it. Your iris — it does not change. And if security forces arrest you and capture your iris data, the government that arrested you will not forget.

UNRWA said: the data is protected. It is encrypted. Governments do not have access. But trust in digital systems does not rest on promises — it rests on physics. And the physics of digital security: if a government wants access, it will get it.

Palestine is not an ordinary case. Israeli security has pressured UNRWA repeatedly for access to refugee data. Legally, UNRWA cannot give it to them. Practically — the pressure continues.

IrisGuard specifically — UNRWA chose an American company called EyeID. The company said: data is secure. UNRWA said: approved. But no one gave them permission to see how security works underneath.

## Why this matters

You are collecting food assistance. UNRWA says: we need a photograph of your iris. You say: no. The result: you do not receive the service. A choice? Barely — without this assistance, your family does not eat.

This is the choice between data and survival. And the government — any government — knows that the poor have no choice.

Biometrics is not neutral technology. It is technology directed toward the observer — the entity that holds the files, the photographs, the decision over access.

Read UNRWA's terms and conditions. See how they treat data. And ask: if the occupation demanded access — would they say no?
