You open ChatGPT and write a question in Arabic. You press enter. The model responds in Modern Standard Arabic — grammatically correct, but strange. Like someone outside the world is talking back to you. It asks you about "future horizons for technological industry in the region" when you are asking it what the cheapest iPhone costs in Jerusalem.

The model was trained on billions of words. Among them — just a small portion — Arabic texts. Most of them are Modern Standard Arabic from news and Wikipedia. Little of it is dialect. Almost nothing is Palestinian everyday conversation.

The result: the model understands Modern Standard Arabic better than dialect. When you write it a request phrased in formal Arabic, it answers correctly. When you write to it in real Palestinian Arabic, it stumbles.

Try the model on a sentence like "what are the stories from behind the scenes?" It will respond with information about conspiracy theories. Try it on the same idea but in Palestinian dialect — something the Arabic speaker would naturally say — and it says "I apologize, I do not understand your question clearly."

Not because the model is stupid. Because the training did not include this conversation in the right form.

The problem goes deeper. The model names things by names you do not use. It tells you "the musical group" when you ask about the band. It says "personal computing device" when you ask about your laptop. It writes you formal Arabic even when you ask for natural conversation.

Arabic in large language models is not a living language. It is filtered language — stripped of errors, colloquialisms, dialect. When you use it, you feel the difference.

The solution is not simple. It requires balance between formal, colloquial, and professional registers. Newer models try to improve this. But today, when you write Arabic to the model, expect that it does not understand you completely, especially if you write in your actual language.

And the most dangerous part: the model speaks with confidence. Meaning even when it does not understand, it responds as if it does. A set of questions you will need to ask in English to get the correct answer.

---

If you are using ChatGPT:
- Write in formal Arabic if you want reliable answers
- Use English for sensitive information
- Verify answers, especially in Arabic

The model is not against the Arabic language. The model was just not trained on it with the same quality.
